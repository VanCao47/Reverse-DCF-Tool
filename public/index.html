Assumptions

Why 9%?

Approximates the long-run average annual return of the broad stock market, used as a default "required return" for a roughly average-risk company. Fetch a ticker above to get a company-specific estimate via CAPM (risk-free rate + beta × equity risk premium) — riskier, higher-beta companies land higher in the range (up to ~11%+), stable low-beta ones lower (~8%).

Why 3%?

This is the rate cash flow is assumed to grow forever, so it's conventionally capped near long-run economy-wide growth — a company growing faster than the whole economy forever eventually becomes larger than the economy. 3% approximates long-run US nominal GDP growth: roughly 2% inflation (the Fed's target) plus ~1% real growth. Because terminal value is often 50-75%+ of total DCF value, this input matters a lot — that's why the sensitivity table sweeps it by ±1%.

Why 10 years?

A common stage-1 window: long enough for an elevated growth phase to plausibly fade toward maturity, short enough that explicit year-by-year assumptions still feel grounded. Longer fade periods (15-20y) suit early-stage/high-growth companies with more "excess growth" to work off; shorter ones (5y) suit already-mature, slow-growth companies.
